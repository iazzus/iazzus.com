Tesla

Daily transport, charged at home, and the first vehicle I have owned whose behaviour changes overnight without anyone touching it. That turns out to be the interesting part.

The car

Transport as infrastructure

An electric car is closer to a piece of managed infrastructure than to a traditional vehicle. It has a firmware version, a release channel, telemetry, an account attached to it, and a manufacturer who can change how it behaves without asking. Some of that is genuinely excellent and some of it is worth thinking about carefully.

The practical side is simpler than people expect. It charges overnight at home, it leaves every morning with more range than a commute needs, and the maintenance list is short enough to be memorised: tyres, cabin filter, brake fluid, wiper blades.

What replaces fuel stops is planning, and what replaces engine servicing is paying attention to battery health and tyre wear.

Running it

Charging, efficiency, cost

01

Home charging

Overnight charging on a schedule that targets off-peak rates. The car is full every morning, which removes the single biggest objection people have to electric vehicles.

02

Efficiency

Consumption tracked over time rather than per trip. Weather, tyre pressure and driving style all move the number more than anything the car does on its own.

03

Real cost

Cost per mile from actual electricity rates, not from a manufacturer estimate. Tyres are the running cost people underestimate on a heavy, torquey car.
