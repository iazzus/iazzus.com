Life

Reptiles

Husbandry is a monitoring problem with a living dependency. Get the gradient, the humidity and the feeding interval right and almost everything else takes care of itself. Get them wrong slowly and you will not notice until it matters.

Approach

Instrument everything

Every enclosure gets measured rather than estimated. Surface temperature at the basking spot and at the cool end, ambient humidity, and a thermostat with a backup cutoff on anything that produces heat. A thermometer that reads one number for the whole enclosure is not telling you what you need to know.

Feeding, sheds, weights and defecation go into a log. The value is not any single entry — it is being able to answer “is this normal for this animal?” with evidence instead of a guess.

This is the same discipline as the rest of the site, applied to something that cannot tell you when it is uncomfortable.

Husbandry

What actually gets checked

01

Thermal gradient

A measured warm end and cool end, so the animal can choose its own temperature. Checked at the surface, not in the air.

02

Humidity

Held in the range the species evolved for, with substrate and ventilation doing the work rather than daily misting.

03

Lighting

Appropriate UVB where the species needs it, on a fixed photoperiod, replaced on schedule — output falls long before the bulb stops lighting up.

04

Records

Weights, feeds, sheds and behaviour, dated. A trend line is the earliest warning you get that something is wrong.
