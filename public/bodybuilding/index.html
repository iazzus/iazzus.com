Development on a schedule.

I am building toward Men's Physique one training block at a time. Right now I am in a growth phase, adding size where the division rewards it most, improving weak points, and keeping the process measurable enough to know whether the work is actually working.

Priorities

What the physique is being built around

Six priorities. Everything in a session has to justify itself against one of them.

01

Delts

Width changes the entire silhouette. Side and rear delt development remain central to creating the shoulder-to-waist contrast Men's Physique rewards.

02

Chest

More overall chest development, with particular attention to the upper chest so the torso looks complete from the front rather than simply lean.

03

Arms

Enough size to match the torso and shoulders. Biceps and triceps need to contribute to the silhouette instead of disappearing beside it.

04

Back width

Lat development creates the frame. Width from the front and rear remains one of the defining visual characteristics of the division.

05

Back thickness

A major priority. Width gives the outline. Thickness gives the physique depth, density, and a more complete look from every angle.

06

Proportion

No body part exists in isolation. The objective is not to maximize everything independently. It is to build a physique where the pieces make sense together.

Training

Push. Pull. Legs.

My training is built around a traditional Push / Pull / Legs rotation. It is not complicated for the sake of being complicated. Priority movements go where I can perform them well, enough productive volume gets distributed across the week, and the goal is to gradually make the work harder without allowing execution to deteriorate.

Exercises do not get replaced simply because I am bored with them. Movements earn their place by giving me something I can execute, progress, and recover from.

If something stops producing what I need from it, I change it. Otherwise, I keep working.

Philosophy

The rules I train by

  1. 01

    Progression is the program

    More weight is one form of progression, not the only one. More reps, better control, improved range of motion, stronger execution, and greater output from the target tissue all count.

  2. 02

    Technique sets the ceiling

    Loading a movement means very little if the intended muscle is no longer doing the work. Execution determines whether progression is productive or merely measurable.

  3. 03

    Recovery is part of training

    The session creates the stimulus. Food, sleep, fatigue management, and time determine what I can actually recover from and build upon.

  4. 04

    Measure, then decide

    One difficult session, one bad weigh-in, or one good pump does not justify rewriting the plan. Patterns matter more than individual data points.

Nutrition

Food has to work in real life.

Nutrition supports the objective, but the plan still has to survive the rest of my life.

Protein stays consistent. Calories reflect the current phase. Bodyweight and visual changes are watched as trends rather than judged from a single morning.

I prefer meals that are repeatable enough to measure and flexible enough that eating does not become a second full-time job.

The objective is not to eat perfectly. It is to eat predictably enough that I can tell what the plan is doing.

Progress

Documented, not remembered.

Memory is terrible at judging gradual change.

Photographs, bodyweight trends, training performance, and repeated check-ins provide something better: a record taken under the same conditions each time, which makes the comparison mean something.

As this physique develops, this becomes the record. Not only of what improved, but of what did not, what changed, and what I learned from it.

Training built around your goal and the equipment you actually have. Commercial gym, apartment gym, garage setup, home equipment, limited machines, dumbbells only. The program should reflect reality.

Practical meal structure based around your objective, preferences, schedule, budget, and the food you can actually get.

About my approach

Built while doing it myself.

I am not documenting a finished physique from the other side of the process. I am in it.

I train for Men's Physique and I am in a growth phase, working through the same basic variables I ask anyone else to respect: training, food, recovery, consistency, measurement, and patience.

My approach tends to be analytical. Establish the baseline. Change what needs changing. Give the change enough time to produce evidence. Keep what works.

A decade of technical work probably explains part of the mentality. I am much more interested in identifying what is actually failing than in repeatedly replacing the entire system.

That is how I approach my own development, and it is the foundation of how IAZZUS coaching works.
