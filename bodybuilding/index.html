Men's Physique

Development on a schedule

Men's Physique is judged on shape and balance, not on how much weight moved. The training reflects that: specific targets, honest measurement, and revision when the evidence says the plan is not working.

Current focus

Where the work is going right now

The current block prioritises upper-body structure — delts, chest and arms — with dedicated back work split between width and thickness rather than treated as one thing. Lower body is trained to support proportion, not to compete with it.

Conditioning is managed gradually. Losing size to reveal shape is a bad trade, so the target is slow enough that strength holds.

Emphasis

What the physique is being built around

Six priorities. Everything in a session has to justify itself against one of them.

01

Shoulders

Lateral and rear development for the width that defines the division. High frequency, controlled range, no ego loading.

02

Chest

Full development across upper and mid regions, trained through angles rather than through a single pressing pattern.

03

Arms

Biceps and triceps trained directly and deliberately. In a board-shorts division they are visible in every pose.

04

Back width

Vertical pulling for the taper that carries the front and rear presentation. Range and lat engagement over load.

05

Back thickness

Horizontal pulling for mid-back and rear detail — the half of back training that usually gets skipped.

06

Conditioning & symmetry

A waist and body-fat level that keeps the proportion readable, with left-to-right balance checked in photographs, not in mirrors.

Training philosophy

Four rules that do most of the work

  1. 01

    Progression is the programme

    Load, reps or quality has to move over a block. Novelty is not progress, and a session that cannot be compared to the last one is a session that cannot be improved.

  2. 02

    Technique sets the ceiling

    Execution decides which muscle receives the work. Controlled range, deliberate tempo and honest positioning beat a heavier number that the target muscle never sees.

  3. 03

    Recovery is part of the plan

    Sleep, food and managed volume are inputs, not afterthoughts. Training beyond what can be recovered from produces fatigue, not development.

  4. 04

    Measure, then decide

    Bodyweight, training logs and photographs taken under the same conditions. Decisions come from the trend line, not from how a given day felt.

Training and nutrition

Training

How sessions are structured

Each session opens with the priority movement for that day while fatigue is lowest, then moves outward to supporting work and finishes with isolation for whichever region is being emphasised in the current block.

Volume is distributed across the week rather than concentrated into one heroic session per muscle group, and everything is logged: exercise, load, reps and the note that explains why a number changed.

Nutrition

How food is managed

Protein is held constant, calories are adjusted against the bodyweight trend rather than a single morning reading, and food choices stay repeatable enough to be tracked accurately without becoming miserable.

Adjustments are made on a schedule, not on impulse. If the trend has not moved for long enough to be meaningful, something changes; if it has, nothing does.

Progress

Documented, not remembered

Check-ins recorded on the same day of the week, in the same conditions, so the comparison actually means something.

Goals

What this is aimed at

Near term

Complete the current development block with measurable progress in shoulder and upper-back development, without losing conditioning.

Medium term

Build a physique that holds proportion at a lower body-fat level — the point where a stage-ready presentation becomes realistic.

Long term

Compete in Men's Physique, and keep a documented record of the process that got there rather than just the result.
